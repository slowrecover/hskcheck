八段锦Baduanjin Follow-Along

Baduanjin, the Eight Brocades, is a gentle Chinese qigong routine of eight slow movements. Press start and follow the figure: it moves with your breath, counts the rounds and tells you what comes next.

    What is Baduanjin?

    Baduanjin (八段锦, bā duàn jǐn) means "eight pieces of brocade": eight movements as fine as silk. It is one of the oldest and most widely practised forms of qigong, done slowly, with relaxed breathing and a calm mind. Many people in China practise it in parks every morning, and it needs no equipment and very little space.

    This page follows the Health Qigong version promoted by China's General Administration of Sport: an opening, eight movements and a closing. In May 2026 the China Association of Chinese Medicine and the Chinese Health Qigong Association published the first group standard for Baduanjin, based on the same version.

    Each movement has a traditional name that describes, in the language of Chinese medicine, what it was believed to do. The translations below are only to explain the names. Baduanjin is gentle exercise, not medical treatment.

    The movements, step by step

    Opening

    预备势 Opening position

    yùbèi shì · Getting ready

    1. Stand with your feet together and arms by your sides, then step your left foot out to shoulder width.
    2. Bend your knees a little. Raise your arms out to the sides and bring them round in front of your navel, palms facing you, as if holding a large ball.
    3. Relax your shoulders and elbows, keep your head up and look straight ahead. Breathe naturally.

    Do once, then breathe calmly for a moment. Keep your knees behind your toes and your lower back relaxed.

    Movement 1

    两手托天理三焦 Holding up the sky

    liǎng shǒu tuō tiān lǐ sān jiāo · Both hands hold up the sky to regulate the Triple Burner

    1. Interlace your fingers in front of your belly, palms up.
    2. Lift your hands to your chest, turn the palms over and push them up above your head until your arms are straight. Glance up at your hands, then look ahead and hold the stretch for a moment.
    3. Let go, lower your arms out to the sides and bend your knees a little, bringing your hands back in front of your belly.

    6 times. Stretch up through the whole body, but keep your shoulders down.

    Movement 2

    左右开弓似射雕 Drawing the bow

    zuǒ yòu kāi gōng sì shè diāo · Draw the bow left and right like shooting an eagle

    1. Step out into a wide horse stance and cross your wrists in front of your chest.
    2. Push one hand out to the side at shoulder height, index finger pointing up, while the other hand pulls back to the opposite shoulder as if drawing a bow. Look at the pushing hand.
    3. Open your hands, bring them back in front of your chest and do the other side. Left and right make one round.

    3 rounds, each round once to each side. Keep your upper body upright and both arms in one line, like a real bow.

    Movement 3

    调理脾胃须单举 Raising one arm

    tiáolǐ píwèi xū dān jǔ · Raise one arm to regulate the spleen and stomach

    1. Start with both hands in front of your belly.
    2. Raise one hand past your face, turn the palm up and push it above your head. At the same time press the other hand down beside your hip, fingers forward.
    3. Stretch between the two hands for a moment, then bring both back in front of your belly and change sides.

    3 rounds, each round once to each side. Push up and down with the same strength, keeping your body straight.

    Movement 4

    五劳七伤往后瞧 Looking back

    wǔ láo qī shāng wǎng hòu qiáo · Look back to ease the five strains and seven injuries

    1. Straighten your legs with your arms hanging by your sides.
    2. Turn your arms so the palms face out, and slowly turn your head to look back over one shoulder. Keep your body facing forward.
    3. Turn your head back to the front, bend your knees a little and press your palms down beside your hips. Then look back over the other shoulder.

    3 rounds, each round once to each side. Only your head turns. If your neck is stiff, turn just as far as is comfortable.

    Movement 5

    摇头摆尾去心火 Swaying the head and tail

    yáo tóu bǎi wěi qù xīn huǒ · Sway the head and swing the tail to clear heart fire

    1. Step into a wide horse stance and rest your hands on your thighs just above the knees.
    2. Lean your upper body down to one side, then sweep it round in a low arc to the other side, with your head and tailbone moving in opposite directions.
    3. Come back up to the centre and repeat the other way. One sweep each way is one round.

    3 rounds, each round once to each side. Move from the hips, smoothly and slowly. Make the lean smaller if you have back problems.

    Movement 6

    两手攀足固肾腰 Reaching down to the feet

    liǎng shǒu pān zú gù shèn yāo · Both hands reach the feet to strengthen the kidneys and waist

    1. Raise your arms above your head, palms forward. Lower your hands to your chest, palms down.
    2. Slide your hands under your arms and round to your back, then down along your spine and the backs of your legs as you bend forward, until they reach your feet.
    3. Slide your hands forward over your feet and rise up slowly, arms leading, until you are standing with your arms above your head.

    6 times. Keep your knees straight but not locked, and only bend as far as is comfortable. Skip this movement if bending forward is not safe for you.

    Movement 7

    攒拳怒目增气力 Punching with a fierce gaze

    cuán quán nù mù zēng qìlì · Clench the fists and glare to build strength

    1. Step into a horse stance with both fists at your waist, thumbs tucked in.
    2. Slowly punch one fist forward at shoulder height. Open your eyes wide and look at your fist, gripping the ground with your toes.
    3. Open the hand, turn the wrist in a circle and grasp, then pull the fist back to your waist. Change sides.

    3 rounds, each round once to each side. The punch is slow and firm, not fast. Keep your body upright.

    Movement 8

    背后七颠百病消 Bouncing on the toes

    bèi hòu qī diān bǎi bìng xiāo · Seven jolts at the back to send away a hundred illnesses

    1. Stand with your feet together and your arms by your sides.
    2. Rise up onto your toes, lift your head and hold for a moment.
    3. Let your heels drop lightly to the ground with a small jolt, relaxing your whole body. Do it seven times.

    7 times. Drop the heels softly. Hold on to a chair if your balance is not steady.

    Closing

    收势 Closing

    shōu shì · Finishing the set

    1. Raise your arms out to the sides, palms facing back.
    2. Bring your hands together over your lower belly, one on top of the other, and look down. Breathe calmly for a few breaths.
    3. Lower your arms to your sides and relax.

    Do once, then breathe calmly for a moment. Take a moment to stand still before you walk away.

    Safety. Move within a range that feels comfortable and stop if anything hurts. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, back or joint problems, or are pregnant, check with a doctor first. The bending and bouncing movements can be made smaller or skipped.

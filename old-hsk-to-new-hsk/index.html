How the old and new HSK lists compare

Why old HSK words don't carry over one to one

The old HSK had six levels and about 5,000 words. HSK 3.0 has nine levels, and the 2025 syllabus spreads its words differently: many words move to a lower level, many are new, and some old words are gone. A pass at old HSK 4 does not mean you know the words of new HSK 4.

An example

New HSK 1–4 has 1,978 words. Someone who studied all of old HSK 1–4 has met 1,120 of them, so 858 are still to learn. 96 of those use at least one character that never appeared in the old HSK 1–4 words.

"New characters" and "familiar characters"

Many words that are new on paper are built from characters you already know. The old list has 这 and 个, the new one lists 这个 as its own word. These go under "familiar characters" and are usually quick to pick up. Words with a character you have never studied go under "new characters": start there.

How it works. Each word of the new HSK levels you pick is looked up in the old HSK lists. If it appears at or below the old level you chose, it counts as met. Words are compared as written, so a word only counts as met if the old list has that exact word. A word ending in 儿 also matches the same word without it, and a word that is part of an old entry counts as met (篮球 in 打篮球).

    What is Baduanjin?

    Baduanjin (八段锦, bā duàn jǐn) means "eight pieces of brocade": eight movements as fine as silk. It is one of the oldest and most widely practised forms of qigong, done slowly, with relaxed breathing and a calm mind. Many people in China practise it in parks every morning, and it needs no equipment and very little space.

    This page follows the Health Qigong version promoted by China's General Administration of Sport: an opening, eight movements and a closing. In May 2026 the China Association of Chinese Medicine and the Chinese Health Qigong Association published the first group standard for Baduanjin, based on the same version.

    Each movement has a traditional name that describes, in the language of Chinese medicine, what it was believed to do. The translations below are only to explain the names. Baduanjin is gentle exercise, not medical treatment.

    Safety. Move within a range that feels comfortable and stop if anything hurts. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, back or joint problems, or are pregnant, check with a doctor first. The bending and bouncing movements can be made smaller or skipped.

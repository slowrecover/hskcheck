How Chinese measure words work

In Chinese, a number can't go straight before a noun. A measure word (量词, liàngcí), also called a classifier, sits between them: 一只猫 (a cat), 三本书 (three books). The same goes for 这, 那 and 几: 这本书, 几个人.

Most measure words group nouns by shape or kind. 条 is for long, narrow things like rivers, roads and trousers; 张 is for flat things like paper, tables and tickets; 只 is for most animals.

个 is the general measure word. If you don't know the right one, 个 is often understood, but using the specific word sounds far more natural.

To say "two", use 两, not 二, before a measure word: 两个人, 两杯茶.

了How to Use 了 (le) in Chinese

了 (le) is a small word with two main jobs. After a verb, it shows that an action is finished. At the end of a sentence, it shows that something has changed or is new. It is one of the first grammar points of HSK 1.

了 after the verb: a finished action

Subject + verb + 了 + object.

Put 了 straight after the verb to say the action happened and is complete. This works best when the object has a number or a describing word in front of it, such as 三本书 or 很多东西. A short object on its own, as in 我买了书, often sounds unfinished, as if more is coming.

  1. 我买了三本书。Wǒ mǎi le sān běn shū.I bought three books.
  2. 她买了很多东西。Tā mǎi le hěn duō dōngxi.She bought lots of things.
  3. 我们看了一个电影。Wǒmen kàn le yí ge diànyǐng.We watched a film.

了 at the end: something has changed

When 了 comes at the end of the sentence, it tells the listener that the situation is new: it has started raining, it is now eight o'clock, you have changed your plans. This 了 can also follow an adjective, as in 天冷了, or a negative with 不, as in 我不想去了.

With a simple verb and object, such as 吃饭 or 睡觉, putting 了 at the end is the natural way to say it has happened.

  1. 下雨了。Xià yǔ le.It's raining now.
  2. 天冷了。Tiān lěng le.It's got cold.
  3. 他今年十岁了。Tā jīnnián shí suì le.He's ten this year.
  4. 八点了，我们去学校吧。Bā diǎn le, wǒmen qù xuéxiào ba.It's eight o'clock, let's go to school.
  5. 我不想去了。Wǒ bù xiǎng qù le.I don't want to go any more.
  6. 他睡觉了。Tā shuìjiào le.He's gone to bed.

Two 了 together: up to now

With a length of time, you can use 了 in both places. The action began in the past and is still going on now.

  1. 我学了一年中文了。Wǒ xué le yì nián Zhōngwén le.I've been learning Chinese for a year.
  2. 我在这儿住了三年了。Wǒ zài zhèr zhù le sān nián le.I've lived here for three years.

Negatives and questions

To say an action did not happen, use 没有 before the verb and drop 了. A very common mistake is to keep 了 and say 他没有来了 instead of 他没有来. Use 还没有 for "not yet".

To ask whether something has happened, add 吗 after 了, or end the question with 了没有. Answer yes with the verb and 了, or no with 还没有.

  1. 他没有来。Tā méiyǒu lái.He didn't come.
  2. 我还没有吃饭。Wǒ hái méiyǒu chī fàn.I haven't eaten yet.
  3. 你吃饭了吗？Nǐ chī fàn le ma?Have you eaten?
  4. 他回家了没有？Tā huí jiā le méiyǒu?Has he gone home?

了 is not a past tense

Learners often add 了 to everything that happened in the past. Chinese does not need it there. If you are describing how things were, or something you did regularly, a time word such as 昨天 or 去年 is enough.

  1. 我昨天很忙。Wǒ zuótiān hěn máng.I was busy yesterday.
  2. 去年我在中国工作。Qùnián wǒ zài Zhōngguó gōngzuò.Last year I worked in China.

    The three tone changes every learner needs

    1. Third tone before third tone

    When two third tones come together, the first one is said as a second tone: 你好 is written nǐ hǎo but said ní hǎo, and 可以 is written kěyǐ but said kéyǐ. In a longer run of third tones, every one except the last usually rises: 我也很好 is said wó yé hén hǎo at normal speed.

    2. 一 (yī)

    一 keeps its first tone when you count, in dates and numbers, and at the end of a word: 一、二、三, 第一, 统一. Before a fourth tone it becomes second tone: 一定 yídìng, 一个 yí ge. Before the other tones it becomes fourth tone: 一起 yìqǐ, 一天 yì tiān. Between two copies of a verb it is light: 看一看 kàn yi kàn.

    3. 不 (bù)

    不 becomes second tone before a fourth tone: 不是 búshì, 不要 búyào. Before other tones it stays bù: 不好 bù hǎo. In a yes-no question like 去不去 it is light: qù bu qù.

    Why doesn't pinyin show the changes?

    Dictionaries write the original tone so you always know a character's own tone. Textbooks are mixed: many mark the changes for 一 and 不 but not for third tones. That is why the same word can look different in two books. This page shows both, so you can see which is which.
